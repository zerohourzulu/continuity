CONTINUITY
A Durable Authority and Continuity Layer for Autonomous AI
Agents
Working Title — Project Continuity
Version 0.1 — Founding Vision
27 August 2026
1. Executive Thesis
AI agents are becoming capable of acting in the world.
They can communicate, negotiate, purchase services, call APIs, hire other agents, operate wallets, execute
workflows, modify infrastructure, and increasingly act without a human continuously present.
But an agent capable of action is not yet an institutional actor.
An agent may possess an identity without having legitimate authority.
It may possess money without having permission to spend it.
It may perform a task without leaving durable evidence of why it was allowed to perform that task.
It may delegate work to another agent without clearly transmitting—or limiting—the authority under which
the first agent itself was operating.
It may disappear while contracts, duties, funds, relationships, promises and liabilities created during its
existence remain.
Today's agent infrastructure is rapidly solving important pieces of this problem: identity, authentication,
reputation, payment, signed mandates, commerce and delegated capabilities.
Continuity addresses what remains between those pieces.
Its purpose is to make the following questions mechanically answerable:
Who is this actor?
Who empowered it?
What exactly may it do?
1

=== SOURCE PAGE 002 ===
How did that authority reach it?
What restrictions travel with that authority?
What has it committed itself—or its principal—to do?
Who is accountable for the action?
What happens to its duties and powers if it disappears?
Continuity is therefore not primarily an identity protocol, payment protocol, wallet, agent framework or
blockchain application.
It is a durable authority, relationship, obligation and succession layer for autonomous actors.
Its fundamental object is not the agent.
Its fundamental object is the graph of authority and responsibility in which the agent exists.
2. The Problem
Human civilization solved the problem of ephemeral individuals acting in a persistent world long ago.
A person may die, resign, become incapacitated or simply leave.
Nevertheless:
• corporations continue;
• offices continue;
• contracts continue;
• debts continue;
• trusts continue;
• estates continue;
• titles and roles can pass to successors;
• authority can be delegated;
• authority can be revoked;
• powers can be limited;
• records survive their creators;
• property can be held separately from the person operating it;
• responsibility can be attributed;
• disputes can examine provenance;
• institutions can outlive every person who created them.
These mechanisms allow transient human beings to create durable action.
2

=== SOURCE PAGE 003 ===
AI agents have almost none of this structure by default.
An agent today can be:
• instantiated in seconds;
• duplicated;
• forked;
• renamed;
• upgraded;
• replaced by another model;
• disconnected from its original principal;
• given a wallet;
• given credentials;
• granted broad API permissions;
• allowed to create subagents;
• terminated without a successor;
• recreated later with uncertain continuity.
The resulting ambiguity will become increasingly dangerous as the economic value controlled by
autonomous software increases.
The central problem is therefore not:
How do we make AI agents autonomous?
We are already doing that.
The deeper problem is:
How do we make autonomous action durable, bounded, attributable and governable?
3. The Market Has Already Validated the Premise
Continuity should not attempt to replace the infrastructure now emerging around autonomous agents.
ERC-8004 provides blockchain-based agent identity, reputation and validation mechanisms. AP2 introduces
cryptographically signed mandates representing user authorization. x402 supplies a machine-native
payment mechanism. ERC-8183 addresses agentic commerce. Multiple current IETF Internet-Drafts are
attacking verifiable agent identity, delegation and capability attenuation. NIST has launched an AI Agent
Standards Initiative centered on secure, interoperable autonomous agents.
This activity is not evidence that Continuity is too late.
It is evidence that the substrate is arriving.
3

=== SOURCE PAGE 004 ===
The wrong strategy would be to create:
• another wallet;
• another identity NFT;
• another reputation protocol;
• another payment rail;
• another generalized agent framework;
• another proprietary authentication system.
Continuity should instead compose with these systems.
Identity standards answer:
Who are you?
Payment protocols answer:
How can value move?
Mandates answer:
What did the user request?
Delegation systems answer:
What capability was granted?
Commerce protocols answer:
How can a job be contracted and settled?
Continuity asks a different class of questions:
Where does this authority live within a persistent structure, what relationships and
obligations did its exercise create, and what happens next?
4. The Core Insight: Power Has Provenance
The most important abstraction in Continuity is Power Provenance.
No consequential autonomous action should exist in isolation.
An action should have a discoverable path such as:

6. Design Principle: Separate the Actor from the
Office
One of the most important architectural choices is to stop treating an AI agent as the container for
everything associated with it.
Humans learned centuries ago that person and office must be separable.
Continuity applies the same principle.
An agent identity may disappear.
A role need not.
An agent runtime may crash.
An obligation must not.
A model may be replaced.
An institution need not change.
A wallet operator may change.

7. Core Protocol Objects
7.1 Principal
A Principal is an entity from which authority ultimately originates.
It may represent:
• a human;
• an organization;
• a multisignature body;
• a DAO;
• another recognized institutional entity.
High-value authority should normally have a traceable root principal.
Continuity should also permit experimental autonomous institutions without human roots, but these
should be visibly distinguishable rather than silently treated as equivalent.
7.2 Agent
An Agent is a durable identity representing an autonomous actor.
An Agent is not the running process itself.
It may survive:
• model upgrades;
• machine migration;
• software replacement;
• key rotation;
• temporary shutdown;
• restoration from backup.
An agent therefore resembles an institutional identity more than a UNIX process.

7.3 Runtime

This solves a major semantic error common to agent systems:

7.4 Role
A Role is a durable office containing powers and responsibilities.
Examples:

Researcher
Buyer
Auditor
Customer-Support-Agent
Portfolio Analyst

Authority should often belong to the Role rather than directly to the Agent.
An Agent occupies a Role.

=== SOURCE PAGE 010 ===
When the Agent leaves, another Agent can occupy it.
That makes institutional continuity natural.
7.5 Authority
Authority describes what an entity is empowered to do.
Authority can contain constraints including:
• action type;
• resource;
• monetary amount;
• counterparty;
• geographic jurisdiction;
• time interval;
• invocation count;
• data classification;
• API;
• risk class;
• required co-signers;
• ability to subdelegate;
• maximum delegation depth.
Authority should be attenuating:
A child delegation cannot create power that its parent did not possess.
7.6 Mandate
A Mandate is a bounded instruction issued under authority.
Example:
Find replacement SSDs
Maximum total spend: $400
Delivery deadline: Friday
Approved merchants: X, Y, Z
May create research subagents: yes
May delegate payment authority: no
Continuity should interoperate with mandate formats such as AP2 rather than unnecessarily inventing
incompatible authorization semantics.

7.8 Obligation
An Obligation is something that remains true even if the actor that created it disappears.
Examples:

maintain service;

This is crucial.
Most agent systems model capabilities:
What may the agent do?
Continuity must also model duties:
What must still be done?
A durable agent economy requires both.
7.9 Succession Rule
A Succession Rule determines what happens when an actor can no longer perform a role or obligation.
Triggers might include:
• explicit termination;
• key revocation;
• missed heartbeat;
• policy violation;
• expiration;
• insolvency;
• human intervention;
• runtime failure;
• unavailable service endpoint.
Possible responses:
transfer role to Agent C;
return control to Human Alice;
freeze funds;
cancel authority;
preserve obligations;
transfer Contract 42 only;
require human review.
Powers and obligations need not succeed together.

That distinction is essential.
7.10 Receipt
Every consequential action can produce an Authority Receipt.
An Authority Receipt is a compact cryptographically verifiable artifact containing sufficient information to
establish:
WHO acted
WHAT was done
UNDER WHICH role
USING WHICH mandate
DERIVED FROM WHICH authority
UNDER WHICH policy version
WITH WHICH constraints
AT WHAT time
WITH WHAT result
The receipt may contain hashes rather than confidential underlying data.
This allows third parties to verify authority without exposing everything that produced it.
8. A New Primitive: The Agent Charter
Continuity should introduce the concept of an Agent Charter.
A Charter is the durable constitutional document governing an Agent, Role or Institution.
It may specify:
purpose;
permitted activities;
forbidden activities;
root principal;
governance;
delegation policy;
risk limits;
escalation conditions;
succession;
financial controls;

data restrictions;
required validators;
termination rules.
For example:
CHARTER: ResearchOffice-7
PURPOSE:
Acquire technical information.
MAY:
Search public sources.
Purchase data access below $10/request.
Hire research subagents.
MAY NOT:
Purchase physical goods.

9. A Second New Primitive: The Continuity Capsule
Every durable Agent or Role may have a Continuity Capsule.
The capsule answers:
If this actor vanished right now, what must the system know in order to continue safely?

11. Authority Must Include Negative Space
Permissions alone are insufficient.
Continuity should be capable of expressing both:

and

12. Revocation Cascades
Delegation creates descendants.
Revocation must understand them.
Suppose:
Alice
↓
Agent A
↓
Agent B
↓
Agent C
If Alice revokes the authority originally granted to Agent A, downstream powers derived exclusively from
that grant should become invalid automatically.
However, if Agent C independently possesses another valid authority path, that authority should survive.
Therefore validity is computed from the graph rather than from simple ownership.
17

=== SOURCE PAGE 018 ===
This enables:
• cascading revocation;
• partial revocation;
• dependency analysis;
• impact previews.
Before a principal revokes authority, Continuity could answer:
What will break if I revoke this?
That alone could become a valuable enterprise feature.

A durable identity should not behave like an ordinary transferable collectible.
If Agent 382:

• accumulated reputation;
• incurred obligations;
• performed work;
• produced descendants;

Continuity therefore separates:

from:

from:

from:

15. What Goes On-Chain—and What Must Not
Continuity should resist the temptation to put everything on-chain.
The protocol should use blockchain for what blockchain is unusually good at:
• timestamped durable commitments;
• signatures;
• authority transitions;
• ownership/control transitions;
• revocations;
• public attestations;
• settlement;
• immutable history anchors.
It should not casually put on-chain:
• private prompts;
• personal information;
• confidential contracts;
• raw corporate data;
• agent memory;
• proprietary reasoning;
• large files;
• unnecessary operational chatter.
Sensitive objects should remain off-chain.
The chain should contain:
hash
identifier
signature
policy reference
state transition
proof
whenever possible.
Privacy must be structural, not an afterthought.

16. Selective Disclosure
A relying party often needs proof of authority without seeing the entire authority graph.
Example:
A merchant may need to know:
This agent may spend up to $500 on computer equipment today.
The merchant does not need to know:
• every corporate relationship;
• employee identities;
• unrelated mandates;
• the principal's other agents;
• internal policies.
Continuity should ultimately support selective disclosure through mechanisms such as verifiable
credentials, commitments and zero-knowledge techniques where appropriate.
The long-term goal is:
prove sufficient authority without revealing unnecessary structure.
17. Interoperability First
Continuity should be chain-agnostic at the specification level.
The protocol should not require users to care whether the persistence layer is:
• Tezos;
• Etherlink;
• Ethereum;
• Base;
• another EVM chain;
• another suitable ledger.
The first implementation may use Tezos infrastructure.
The standard should not become trapped inside Tezos.

18. Why Etherlink Is an Excellent First
Implementation Target
The proof of concept should be built where the development path is shortest and interoperability is highest.
Etherlink currently offers an unusually useful combination:
• EVM compatibility;
• Solidity tooling;
• Tezos settlement infrastructure;
• low transaction cost;
• existing x402 support through Tez402.
Etherlink 7.0 activated on 21 August 2026 and added a Michelson interface plus native atomic composability
between EVM and Michelson contracts.
Therefore the recommended strategy is:
Build the reference implementation on Etherlink, while designing the protocol as chain-
neutral.

Blockchain

21. Economic Relationships
The original intuition that agents may generate value for their creators or communities remains important.
But Continuity itself should not impose a hereditary transaction tax.
Instead, economic relationships should be explicit protocol objects.
Examples:

10% → data supplier
5% → maintenance pool
5% → public-benefit fund

• explicit;
• inspectable;
• revocable where contractually permitted;
• bounded in duration;

22. Public-Benefit Capability
One powerful later extension is to make beneficiary relationships native to the graph.
An individual, company or autonomous institution could specify:
1% of net agent income → environmental restoration pool
5% → elder-care beneficiary
10% → open-source maintenance
remainder → principal
This should not be mandatory.
But if autonomous systems generate substantial economic output, Continuity could provide transparent
machinery through which part of that output benefits humans who are not technically participating in the
agent economy.
This preserves an important humanitarian possibility:
Autonomous labor need not only concentrate economic power.
The same infrastructure that traces authority can trace benefit.
23. Non-Goals
Continuity should explicitly refuse several seductive ambitions.
Continuity does not create legal personhood.
An AI agent does not become a legal person because a blockchain says so.
Continuity produces evidence of identity, authorization, provenance, commitments and declared
relationships.
Legal consequences remain jurisdiction-dependent.
26

=== SOURCE PAGE 027 ===
Continuity is not an AI model.
It should work with many models and agent frameworks.
Continuity is not a payment network.
It should use existing payment systems.
Continuity is not primarily a reputation system.
Reputation asks whether an agent is trustworthy.
Authority asks whether the agent is empowered.
A highly reputable agent may have zero authority to spend my money.
A new agent may have legitimate authority despite having zero reputation.
These concepts must never be conflated.
Continuity is not an ontology of civilization.
We should not attempt to encode every human relationship in version 1.
The core protocol should supply a few composable primitives from which richer relationships can evolve.

Amendment 003
Runtime Liveness,
Recovery, and Control Integrity
RATIFIED FOUNDING AUTHORITY
Ratified by: Project founder
Effective: 28 August 2026
Applies to the Continuity Constitution Version 0.1, as amended
Continuity preserves and verifies durable authority state. External
operational infrastructure supplies liveness and execution.
Current control must be distinguishable from stale control. Recovery and succession follow declared rules; uncertain
external effects remain visible until reconciled.
Amendment page 1

=== SOURCE PAGE 058 ===
Purpose
Continuity distinguishes a durable Agent from the ephemeral Runtime processes that implement it. That distinction is
necessary but incomplete unless the protocol also addresses operational failure, recovery, concurrent execution, and
supervisory takeover.
A process that stops responding may still be executing. A replacement supervisor may begin operating while the original
supervisor later returns. Two runtimes may claim the same durable Agent identity, possess stale credentials, retry an
uncertain transaction, or act from different views of the event history. Blockchain ordering can serialize submitted state
transitions, but ordering alone does not determine which runtime currently holds legitimate authority or prevent duplicate
real-world effects.
This amendment establishes the boundary between Continuity's durable authority semantics and the infrastructure that
supplies liveness. It requires stale control to be detectable and rejectable without turning Continuity into a universal
process manager, distributed operating system, or hosting service.
Ratified principle
Continuity preserves and verifies the durable authority state that survives runtime failure. It does not itself
guarantee that an Agent, Runtime, supervisor, storage service, network, or counterparty remains online or
executes required work. Operational infrastructure supplies liveness; Continuity determines whether the
resulting control, recovery, action, and succession claims are valid under the declared history and rules.
Multiple processes may physically claim or implement one durable Agent identity. They must not thereby acquire
duplicate or ambiguous consequential authority. A Continuity deployment must declare its concurrency policy and must
reject consequential action made under superseded, expired, unauthorized, or incompatible control state.
Required distinctions
Continuity must distinguish at least the following concepts:
- Agent: the durable identity and historical actor represented in the authority graph.
- Runtime: an ephemeral process, model, machine, container, or other implementation of an Agent.
- Runtime Session: a uniquely identifiable admission of a Runtime to act for an Agent under bounded credentials,
time, scope, and control state.
- Continuity Supervisor: a local, hosted, institutional, human-operated, or decentralized mechanism that observes
declared operational conditions and may start, stop, restore, replace, or monitor Runtimes when authorized. A
supervisor may also be called a vigil, watchdog, orchestrator, guardian, or recovery service; those labels do not
themselves confer authority.
- Controller: the entity or declared authority arrangement entitled to admit Runtime Sessions or change supervisory
control.
- Control Epoch: a monotonically advancing control generation against which Runtime Sessions and consequential
actions are validated. Advancing an epoch fences prior control state unless a declared rule expressly preserves a
narrower authority.
- Successor: a distinct Agent appointed under a declared succession rule to receive selected roles, powers,
relationships, contracts, obligations, or unresolved work.
These concepts are not interchangeable. A Runtime restart need not create a new Agent. Supervisory failover need not
cause Agent succession. Agent succession must not be disguised as ordinary process recovery.

=== SOURCE PAGE 059 ===
The operational boundary
Continuity Protocol may specify and verify:
- durable identities, roles, mandates, obligations, and authority paths;
- authorized controllers and supervisors;
- Runtime Session admission and bounded session credentials;
- control epochs, leases, fencing, revocation, and succession rules;
- checkpoint commitments and the authoritative history state to which they correspond;
- transaction intents, receipts, replay protection, and reconciliation evidence;
- the validity and freshness of a claimed control or action state; and
- the consequences that declared rules assign to failure, recovery, termination, and succession.
Continuity Protocol does not, merely by recording those rules:
- detect every process or network failure correctly;
- keep a daemon, Agent, Runtime, supervisor, storage provider, or blockchain online;
- launch, host, restart, or terminate software by itself;
- preserve private prompts, memory, files, secrets, or checkpoints without a separately operated availability
mechanism;
- guarantee that fenced or malicious software physically stops executing;
- undo an external action already accepted by a nonconforming system; or
- guarantee performance of an obligation when no capable and authorized actor is available.
A supervisor or other executor performs operational work. Continuity supplies the rules and evidence by which that work
is recognized, rejected, reconstructed, or explained.
Failure suspicion is not termination
Silence is evidence of unavailability, not proof of nonexistence. Missed heartbeats, expired leases, unreachable
endpoints, delayed messages, network partitions, machine suspension, clock disagreement, overloaded execution, and
observer failure may be indistinguishable from process death for some period.

- the conditions that make recovery or takeover eligible;
- the observer or authority permitted to assert those conditions;
- the relevant time source, waiting period, evidence, approvals, and finality threshold;

human review, or another bounded transition; and
- how a mistaken or malicious recovery claim may be challenged, reversed prospectively, or remedied without rewriting
finalized history.

Operational states such as unresponsive, suspected failed, suspended, fenced, terminated, recovered, and superseded
must not be treated as synonyms when their consequences differ.
Concurrency, control epochs, and fencing
The default safety posture for consequential authority is exclusive current control. A deployment may deliberately
authorize multiple concurrent Runtime Sessions only when their scopes, resources, limits, ordering, and conflict
behavior are declared so that concurrency does not silently enlarge aggregate authority.
Every consequential action must be evaluated against current control state. Its evidence should bind, directly or through
a verifiable reference, the Agent, Runtime Session, control epoch, authority path, policy version, relevant event-history
state, expiration or freshness requirement, and replay or transaction-intent identifier.
When a valid takeover or recovery transition advances the control epoch:
1 prior Runtime Sessions become fenced except to the extent an explicit rule preserves a narrower capability;
2 authority paths dependent exclusively on fenced control become invalid;
3 independent authority paths remain independently evaluable and are not erased merely by association;

The protocol need not guarantee that stale software ceases computation or communication. It must make stale

Canonical ordering is necessary but not sufficient. A blockchain or event log may order two competing writes while
accepting both unless the protocol's state-transition rules require the expected current epoch, unused intent, applicable
sequence or nonce, and valid authority state.
Credentials and recovery authority
Possession of an Agent identifier or historical signing key must not alone prove current Runtime control.
Implementations should separate durable recovery or controller keys from epoch-bound Runtime Session credentials
and action-specific capabilities.
Runtime credentials should be bounded by declared scope, duration, epoch, and revocation conditions. A stale Runtime
credential must not defeat fencing merely because its cryptographic signature remains mathematically valid.
Recovery authority is itself authority and must have provenance, constraints, and accountability. A backup supervisor
may assume control only under a declared rule. High-consequence deployments may require multiple independent
approvals, a challenge period, an emergency freeze, or another disclosed control. Continuity does not prescribe one
governance arrangement for all deployments, but undeclared recovery power is not legitimate recovery authority.
Runtime restart, control transfer, and succession
Continuity must preserve three separate cases:
1 Runtime replacement: a new Runtime Session continues the same durable Agent identity under valid current
control.
2 Supervisory control transfer: another authorized supervisor assumes operational control, fences superseded
sessions, and may admit replacement Runtime Sessions without necessarily changing the Agent identity.

Authoritative state and private recovery state
Recovery must distinguish:
1 Authoritative Continuity state: identities, roles, grants, prohibitions, revocations, control epochs, receipts,

2 Private operational state: prompts, memory, files, credentials, working context, model configuration, and
encrypted checkpoints retained under separately declared custody, replication, and recovery rules.
3 External real-world state: payment, vendor, delivery, communication, legal, physical, or other facts maintained by

A private checkpoint may restore operational context; it must not roll authoritative state backward. A checkpoint intended
for recovery should be bound to the Agent, Runtime or session context, control epoch, policy version, and event-history

transitions and reconcile unresolved external effects.
Blockchain commitments can prove checkpoint integrity but cannot recreate unavailable private content. Amendment
002's distinction between integrity and availability remains controlling.
Transaction intents, retries, and uncertain outcomes
Consequential action must not rely on an assumption of exactly-once execution across independent systems. Network
failure may leave it unknown whether an external system accepted an action. Blind retry can duplicate payment,
purchase, communication, delegation, settlement, discharge, or another consequence.
Continuity must support a durable identifier for a declared transaction intent or equivalent consequential action.
Repeated attempts referring to the same intent must be recognizable as retries rather than automatically treated as new
authority to create additional effects.
Implementations must be able to represent uncertainty honestly. A consequential action may be declared, authorized,
submitted, outcome-unknown, confirmed, failed, compensating, disputed, or discharged according to the applicable
external schema and evidence. The precise vocabulary may vary, but an unknown outcome must not be silently
converted into failure or success.
An Authority Receipt proves the authority evidence and recorded result it contains. It does not, without appropriate
external evidence, prove payment settlement, contractual acceptance, physical delivery, subjective performance, or
legal effect. External systems should use idempotency controls, conditional execution, bounded credentials, and
reconciliation where available. Continuity records their evidence and resulting obligations without claiming unilateral
control over them.
Time, freshness, and finality
Leases, heartbeats, mandate expiration, recovery windows, deadlines, and blockchain finality depend on different
notions of time. A deployment must identify the authoritative time or ordering source for each consequential rule and
disclose material clock, finality, reorganization, and observation assumptions.

Continuity evidence must distinguish historical validity from present authority. A receipt may have been valid when

Pending, observed, included, confirmed, and finalized evidence must not be represented as equivalent when the

Safe degradation and reconciliation
When a Runtime cannot establish sufficiently current control, policy, or history state, consequential action must fail
closed unless a separately declared emergency authority applies. A deployment may permit bounded nonconsequential
activity, such as private reasoning or research, during uncertainty, but must not silently permit spending, contracting,
delegation, succession, obligation discharge, or other durable transitions without current authority.
Recovery requires reconciliation as well as replay. A replacing supervisor or Runtime should identify unresolved
transaction intents, submitted but unconfirmed actions, approaching obligations, active descendants, fenced credentials,
external effects absent from its checkpoint, and discrepancies among canonical, local, and external state.
Where a consequence cannot be reversed, the system must preserve evidence of what occurred and apply declared
compensation, dispute, escalation, or obligation rules rather than rewriting history.
Privacy and proportionality
Ordinary heartbeat traffic, Runtime location, infrastructure topology, private checkpoints, and unnecessary operational
telemetry should remain off-chain by default. Public evidence should contain or anchor only what is necessary to verify
consequential control transitions, fencing, authority, and history under the selected deployment profile.
The protocol must not require universal surveillance merely to support continuity. Monitoring, retention, and disclosure
must remain proportionate to the authority and risk being governed.
Honest guarantee
Continuity's claim is not:
Only one process can ever exist, every failure will be detected, and every
obligation will be performed.
Its claim is:
Durable authority and responsibility do not depend on one process continuing to
run. Current control can be distinguished from stale control; recovery and
succession follow declared rules; consequential actions can be rejected when
their authority is superseded; unresolved external effects remain visible for
reconciliation; and the history explains what happened.

Purpose
Continuity is valuable only if its explanations remain honest under incomplete evidence, its obligations and succession
transitions do not imply powers or legal effects they cannot prove, its durability claims distinguish integrity from
retrievability, and its history retains one interpretable lineage through change.
This amendment resolves five bounded ambiguities identified by the independent adversarial review dated 28 August
2026. It does not ratify that review as governing authority and does not add a generalized identity system, workflow
engine, legal system, archival network, distributed operating system, or multi-chain consensus protocol.
1. Scope-bound universal queries
Continuity's universal queries - WHY, RESPONSIBLE, and SURVIVES - return typed evidence claims, not omniscient
or context-free truth.
Every answer must identify, directly or through verifiable references:
● the Continuity deployment and authority domain evaluated;
● the declared root identity or identities and applicable recognition policy;
● the canonical lineage and exact history position or commitment evaluated;
● the applicable protocol, event-schema, and policy versions;
● the finality and freshness state;
● the disclosure scope and any material unavailable, withheld, or private evidence; and
● material external trust, identity, observation, and availability assumptions.
WHY(action) explains why an action was allowed, denied, or could not be determined under the declared authority,
control, and evidence state. It does not establish motive, wisdom, fairness, factual truth, external entitlement, legal
validity, or moral justification.
RESPONSIBLE(action) returns typed declared attribution supported by the available evidence. The types may include
the acting Agent and Runtime Session, occupied Role, mandator, authority sources and approvers, Controller or
Supervisor, declared durable obligor, performance assignee, beneficiary, and any expressly accountable Principal.
Creation, ancestry, approval, control, supervision, or benefit does not by itself imply another attribution type, legal
liability, or moral responsibility.
SURVIVES(actor) returns the recorded roles, authority consequences, relationships, obligations, unresolved intents,
disputes, and other declared state that survives within the evaluated lineage and disclosure scope. It does not claim
knowledge of unavailable private state, unobserved external effects, or duties that were never validly attributed to the
declared system.
An answer whose required evidence is unknown, unavailable, stale, disputed, or conflicting must remain visibly
classified as such. A conforming enforcement point fails closed when it cannot establish a current ALLOW, while the
explanation preserves the difference between a policy denial and an inability to determine current authority.
